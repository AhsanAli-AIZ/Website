AIZ Vault

Your file server, reachable from anywhere your people are.

Teams spread across offices, homes, and countries still need one authoritative place for their documents. AIZ Vault is that place — with access that does not depend on somebody remembering to connect to a VPN first.

The problem

Files sprawl the moment a team stops sharing a building.

It rarely happens deliberately. Someone cannot reach the share from home, so they email a copy. Someone else puts a folder in a personal cloud account to work on a plane. Within a year there are four versions of the important document and no way to tell which one is current.

The fix is not another storage product on top of the ones you have. It is making the authoritative location genuinely easy to reach, so that working around it stops being the path of least resistance.

Shadow copies are a usability symptom, not a discipline problem. People take the shortest path to doing their job.

How it fits together

Three layers, and what each one is responsible for.

Understanding the split matters, because it determines what you control, what is enforced centrally, and where your data physically lives.

The control layer is the point of the product. Storage and clients are commodities; deciding and proving who reached which file is not.

Capabilities

What administrators get.

The people who have to answer for where documents went need more than a file browser. These are the controls that matter when somebody asks the question formally.

Central permissions
Access granted by role and by folder from one place, so removing someone removes them everywhere rather than in six systems.
Access without VPN
Staff reach files from wherever they are working, without a tunnel that has to be connected first and that fails at the worst moments.
Concurrent-edit protection
File locking so two people cannot silently overwrite each other's work on a shared document.
Activity history
A record of who opened, changed, shared, or deleted a file — the thing you need when a client or a regulator asks.
Version recovery
Earlier copies retrievable without going to backup tape, which also covers the most common ransomware scenario.
Regional placement
Where the data physically sits can be chosen to match the rules that apply to your sector and jurisdiction.
